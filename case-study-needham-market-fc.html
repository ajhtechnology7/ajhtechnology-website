The brief

One club. One brand. One digital home.

Needham Market FC is my club. It has a 100-year story and four teams under one badge: the Marketmen, Market Women, the reserves and a full-time academy. It also has a 3,000-capacity ground, a 3G pitch and rooms to hire, sponsors to look after, and supporters who just want to know when the next game is. The job was to give all of that one home that looks like the club, is easy to keep up to date, and lets people ask to book the pitch and rooms online.

What I built

A proper website for a proper club.

A brand refresh, a club website and a booking-request system for the pitch and venue. Tickets and merchandise stay on the club's existing store, and the site links straight to it. I still look after the site, adding matchday programmes and updates as the season goes.

01

Brand refresh

The greyhounds crest stays at the centre. Around it I set out a clear palette (red #C8102E, deep red #8B0A1F, cream #F4EFE3, ink #1B1B22) and three typefaces: Anton, Fraunces italic and DM Sans. It's written up as a short brand guidelines page on the club site.

02

Every team on one site

Pages for the men's first team, Market Women and the reserves, with the full-time academy featured on the homepage and linked to its own site, needhammarketacademy.com. The club's "One Club, one community" line runs through all of it.

03

Fixtures, results & table

Upcoming fixtures, recent results, form and the Southern League Premier Central table on the homepage. The table and results come in automatically from Football Web Pages. I add anything the feed doesn't cover, like county cup ties, by hand.

04

Matchday programmes

A programmes page for the first team and the reserves. I add each new issue as a PDF before the game, so supporters can read it on their phone.

05

News

A news section for match reports and club stories, kept separate from the homepage so older pieces stay easy to find.

06

NeedhamFC TV

The club's YouTube channel, brought into the site. The latest uploads, such as post-match reactions, show on the homepage and link through to the channel.

07

Booking requests

Built in-house for the site. You pick a facility (the 3G pitch, hospitality suite, clubhouse or meeting room), choose a slot on a weekly availability calendar (08:00–22:00, one to four hours) and send a request. The form shows an estimated cost before you send it.

08

Accounts for bookings

You need a free account to request a booking. You verify your email, and you can track your requests from your account. The club checks the diary, confirms by email and invoices you directly. Nothing is paid online.

09

Tickets & shop

Season tickets, match tickets and merchandise are sold through the club's store on KTCKTS. I built the site's shop page, which links out to it. The checkout and payments are KTCKTS's, not mine.

10

Bloomfields & hire

The ground, its stands and what's available to hire, with prices, a booking FAQ, and how matchdays and academy training take priority on the calendar.

11

Partners & sponsorship

A partners page for the club's sponsors, plus pages for matchday sponsorship and the 200 Club, so local businesses can see how to get involved.

12

Keeping it updated

I add matchday programmes, announcements, news and squad changes as the season goes, so the club doesn't have to think about the website.

How it works

A fast static site with one booking system inside it.

Most of the site is simple static pages, which keeps it quick and cheap to run. The two parts that change on their own, the league table and the latest videos, come from outside feeds. The booking requests and accounts are the only part that needs a database.

How the site fits together.
Static pages for content, two outside feeds, a small database for bookings, and links out for the things other services already do well.
Content · Static

Pages, news, squad, programmes

A static site hosted on Netlify and built from a GitHub repository. Announcements, fixtures, news, squad and programmes live in simple data files that I update.

Feed · League

Table, results and form

Pulled automatically from Football Web Pages. If the feed is ever unavailable, the homepage falls back to content I keep up to date by hand.

Feed · Video

NeedhamFC TV

The latest uploads from the club's YouTube channel appear on the homepage, with a link through to the full channel.

Database · Bookings

Availability and requests

A weekly calendar for each facility. It won't let you request a slot that overlaps an existing booking, and every request goes to the club to confirm.

Database · Accounts

Free accounts

Sign up, verify your email and see your booking requests in one place. No card details are taken, because the club invoices you.

Links out

Tickets, shop and academy

Tickets and merchandise open the club's KTCKTS store. The academy links to needhammarketacademy.com. Each runs on its own platform.

How I ran it

Built in stages, kept up since.

I built the site in stages, checking in with the club as I went. Then I kept going: the site is only useful if it stays up to date.

Talk → Brand → Build → Bookings → Keep it updated
Five stages, from first conversation to a site I still update every matchday.
i.

Talking to the club

I talked to people around the club about what the site needed to do, and what could stay with the services the club already used.

  • Every team under one badge: Marketmen, Market Women, reserves, academy
  • Venue hire requests for the 3G pitch and rooms
  • Tickets and merchandise kept on the club's existing store
ii.

Brand refresh

The crest stayed, because that's the point. Around it I set out the colours and typefaces the site uses, and wrote them up as a brand guidelines page the club can point people to.

  • Palette: red #C8102E, deep red #8B0A1F, cream #F4EFE3, ink #1B1B22
  • Type: Anton for headlines, Fraunces italic for accent words, DM Sans for body text
  • "One Club, one community" as the line that runs through the site
iii.

Build

I designed and built the site mobile-first, as a static site on Netlify, with the fixtures, news, squad and programmes in data files that are quick to update.

  • Homepage, team pages, fixtures, news, programmes, partners and welfare
  • League table and results from Football Web Pages; latest videos from YouTube
  • Shop page linking out to the club's KTCKTS store
iv.

Bookings

The one genuinely custom piece: a booking-request system for Bloomfields, built into the site with free accounts behind it.

  • Weekly availability calendar for four facilities, 08:00–22:00
  • Request form with an estimated cost, and free accounts with email verification
  • The club confirms by email and invoices, with no online payments
v.

Keep it updated

A club website goes stale fast in the middle of a season. I keep this one current, so the club can concentrate on the football.

  • Matchday programmes added before each home game
  • Announcements, news and squad changes as they happen
  • Fixes and small improvements when the club asks
Visual system

The club's colours, written down.

The historic crest and colours, set out so they look the same on the website, on social posts and in print. Italic accent words break up the bold uppercase headlines and give the brand a voice: proud, local and welcoming.
